The Manifesto of the Syntō Age is the entry into the system, the start screen of the operating system of a new society. It answers the question: why must humanity leave the model of the struggle for survival and transition to a model of Syntō, pleasure, growth, and co-belonging?

The Manifesto establishes the following:

  • the human being ceases to be a consumer of resources and a cog in production, and becomes aware of oneself as a cell of the unified organism of humanity;
  • the foundation of life becomes the pursuit of pleasure in its highest sense: love, agápe — ἀγάπη, the transmission of knowledge, creation, and participation in the joy of others;
  • Syntōpolis is a step toward the next stage of evolution, not a utopian fantasy. The function of the Manifesto is an emotional, semantic, and value-based initialization. Through it, a person first comes into contact with the concept of Syntō as inner consent and sees that a society is possible in which higher pleasure becomes the norm, not the exception;
  • the Syntō Age is the next stage of evolution, not a utopian fantasy.

All other documents of the Syntō Age and Syntōpolis must be a logical continuation of the Manifesto and must not contradict its spirit.

The Manifesto of the Syntō Age / Syntō

Syntōpolis · 26 Dec 2025

Humanity has entered a phase of extreme intensification of the global political and economic crisis. Models born in an era of struggle for power, resources, and influence have exhausted their developmental potential and are increasingly less capable of providing people with a sense of security, justice, and future perspective. Local and global conflicts, inequality, the fragmentation of societies, and destructive informational noise show that the historically inherited forms of organizing collective human existence no longer correspond to the scale of the challenges and the level of accumulated knowledge.

At the same time, we live in a unique moment in the history of humanity. For the first time, the entire globe has been technically united into a single whole: information networks, the global economy, logistics, and instant communication technologies have transformed the planet into a continuously connected system. Yet this unity remains primarily technical. Humanity still has no common conceptual foundation, no shared image of the future, and no agreed unified ethics corresponding to this new level of interconnectedness and mutual dependence.

It is precisely at this historical moment that a window of opportunity opens. Before us stands an unprecedented task and privilege: to use the experience of all previous systems — religious, philosophical, political, economic, and cultural — not as a field of endless dispute, but as a dataset, as an accumulated library of attempts to build a just and meaningful society. We can regard past models as stages in the evolution of a single experiment and, on this basis, form a new, integral system for organizing the human community.

Today humanity possesses open information, the scientific method, and technologies of collective thinking that allow this choice to be made consciously. Under conditions of such transparency and accumulated experience, the only justified path becomes the path of science and evolution — a path based on verifiable knowledge, honest analysis of errors, and the ability to change structures when data reveal their failure. This path presupposes the use of all the intellectual and creative resources of humanity not for systems to struggle against one another, but for the transition to the next level of organizing life.

If we look at this process on the scale of humanity’s spiritual history, it becomes clear that much of what is now being expressed in the language of science and evolution had already, in its own way, been formulated in teachings directed toward liberation from suffering and the revelation of love as the foundation of being. The Buddha spoke of the path toward the cessation of suffering. Christ affirmed: “God is love.” In the coordinates of the Syntō Age, this vector continues to unfold as agápe — ἀγάπη and the hedonism of higher pleasure: the striving for a state of the world in which pleasure, joy, and co-belonging cease to be the privilege of the few and become the natural background of life within the unified human organism. The fundamental novelty lies in the method of embodiment: today, this centuries-old demand for liberation from suffering and the affirmation of love can be realized without wars and violence — through voluntary Syntō, inner maturation, and the coordinated architecture of a new society.

This transition brings us to a new form of human community. Humanity is conceived as a single living organism, in which every person is a cell — unique, necessary, and possessing one’s own function. The value of each cell is not measured by origin, status, or the volume of accumulated capital, but is determined by its contribution to the health, harmony, and development of the whole. In such an organism, conflicts do not become the primary logic of existence: instead, coordinated mechanisms of self-regulation, coordination, and mutual support are at work. The Syntō Age is a transition from a fragmented set of competing structures to conscious unity, in which personal freedom, cultural diversity, and individual paths of development are preserved and strengthened through inclusion in the common organism.

The essence of further development is the transition from a society of struggle and hierarchies to a society of Syntō, where humanity lives as a unified organism. In practice, this means a unified basic operating system of education for all people, the ethics of hedonism of higher pleasure as the foundation of decision-making, the structuring of society by competencies instead of the power of origin and capital, and the creation of a new habitat in which basic needs are systemically met, while energy is directed toward creation, knowledge, co-belonging, and further evolution.

This transition is not an abstract dream or an ephemeral metaphor. It is conceived as a clearly structured process that can take place only on the basis of goodwill and the conscious inner decision of each person who chooses this path. No external pressure, coercion, or manipulation can give birth to a society of Syntō: it can grow only out of free consent, out of a personal choice to share the values, ethics, and practices of the new society.

It is precisely so that this choice may be made consciously that this book has been written. It is not merely a collection of texts, but a new operating system of society, in which the Syntō Age and Syntōpolis are described as an integral architecture. This system unfolds according to the logic of seven main positions discussed earlier: the manifesto, the constitutional framework, the ethics of hedonism of higher pleasure, the vision of the universal evolution of being, the unified foundation of education, the structuring of society by competencies, and the creation of a new habitat. Together, these seven interconnected modules form the path of transition from a world of struggle and fragmentation to a world of Syntō, pleasure, knowledge, and co-belonging.
